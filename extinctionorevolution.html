Extinction or Evolution?

An honest reader’s report on the opening pages of a nonfiction title.

Start your projectSee all projects

The choice we make today determines the world of tomorrow

My job
Beta reading and developmental editorial critique
Scope
Introduction and start of Chapter 1 (pp. 7–27)
Delivered
8-page PDF report, July 2026
Genre
Nonfiction, technology and society
The book
114 pages, Kindle, Amazon, published July 29, 2026

What came in

  • The Introduction and part of Chapter 1 (pp. 7–27), shared by the author’s team.
  • A nonfiction title about AI and humanity, not a novel.
  • A request for honest feedback from a reader’s and editor’s point of view.
  • Not supplied: the rest of Chapter 1 and everything after it.

What I did

  • Read as a reader first, then as an editor.
  • Swapped the fiction checklist (plot, characters) for nonfiction ones: argument, voice, evidence.
  • Said clearly what was outside the scope instead of guessing.
  • Wrote an 8-page report with strengths, risks, line fixes and a ranked plan.

What the author received

  • A scope note: what was covered and what was not.
  • Strengths and risks, section by section.
  • Notes on voice, internal logic and writing craft.
  • Four line-level rewrite suggestions.
  • A revision plan: 4 high, 3 medium and 2 low priority items.
  • An open offer to talk through any of the feedback.

What I’d fix first

  • Source or soften the exact ImageNet figure.
  • Update the “winter of 2024” line for a 2026 book.
  • Make intentional the pull quote that appears twice.
  • Add one line on the author’s experience, early on.
One detail that matteredThe book was nonfiction, so I replaced the usual plot and character checklist with argument, voice and evidence. The feedback fit the book, not a template. My part was the reader’s report; the cover, layout and publishing were done by the author’s team.
