Line editing: clarity, rhythm and tightening

A line edit works on how each sentence reads: repeated words, slow constructions and hedges that soften claims you clearly believe. I tighten and clarify, and I leave the lines that carry your personality exactly as written.

Copyediting: grammar and consistency

  • Grammar, spelling and punctuation
  • Consistent style, including capitalisation and heading patterns
  • Hedging words that weaken a claim
  • Questions flagged for you where I would otherwise have to guess

What you get

  • Tracked changes with a short reason beside each one
  • A clean manuscript with consistent style
  • The habits I found in your prose, flagged so you can spot them yourself next time

My version

Once I took that first step, I found that learning a skill was harder than I had imagined. Some things came quickly; others demanded patience. On some days I felt I was making progress; on others I wondered whether I was improving at all.

Editor’s comment to the authorThis opening carries the chapter’s emotional shift from excitement to difficulty, so it should move briskly. Your “There were… There were…” pair is accurate but slow. A paired “some / others” shows the same ups and downs in half the space.

Questions about this service

What is the difference between line editing and copyediting?
A line edit improves how sentences read: clarity, rhythm and tightening. A copyedit corrects and standardises: grammar, spelling, punctuation and consistency. I often do both together once the structure is settled.
Will my voice change?
No. I tighten and clarify; I don’t rewrite you. Lines that carry your personality are left exactly as written.
Can I see each change?
Yes. Edits come as tracked changes, each with a short reason, so you decide what to keep.
