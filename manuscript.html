Manuscript assessment and beta reading

An honest read of your manuscript, or part of it, from a reader’s and an editor’s point of view. You get a written report with strengths, risks, line-level suggestions and a ranked revision plan.

Get your free sample editSee my work

How I read

I read as a reader first, then as an editor. For fiction I look at plot, characters, pacing and dialogue. For nonfiction I swap that checklist for argument, voice and evidence, so the feedback fits the book and not a template.

What the report contains

  • A scope note: what I covered and what I did not
  • Strengths and risks, section by section
  • Notes on voice, internal logic and writing craft
  • Line-level rewrite suggestions
  • A revision plan ranked by priority, for example 4 high, 3 medium and 2 low items
  • An open offer to talk through any of the feedback

You can start small

An assessment does not need a finished book. For one nonfiction title I read the Introduction and the opening of Chapter 1 (pages 7 to 27). I say clearly what is outside the scope instead of guessing.

An example from my work

For a nonfiction book about AI and humanity, an 8-page report covered the Introduction and the start of Chapter 1. It named the strongest section, flagged an unsourced statistic and a date that needed updating for a 2026 book, and ended with a ranked plan.

Questions about this service

What is the difference between a beta read and a developmental edit?
A beta read tells you how the book works for a reader: what lands, what drags and what to fix first. A developmental edit goes deeper, with chapter-by-chapter notes and guidance on structure. Many authors start with an assessment, then decide.
Do I need a finished manuscript?
No. I can read an introduction, a few chapters or the whole book. The report states exactly what was covered.
